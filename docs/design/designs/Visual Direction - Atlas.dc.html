01 — The reading

A director who builds worlds, then charts them.

Read the projects as a body of work and the cartographic impulse is everywhere: an animated atlas of Anglo-Saxon kingdoms, theme territories, extraction lineages, a schedule view where time is literally displaced space. He doesn't just model relationships — he maps them.

Atlas takes that at face value. The register is the working document of a surveyor: warm paper, ink, exact plotted marks. Warmth is the rarer claim among engineer portfolios — precision is preserved in the drawing conventions, not the coldness of the palette.

02 — Mood

Three readings considered. Atlas holds.

Signal was eliminated first; Instrument was the strong runner-up, explored in full and rejected on distinctiveness, not rigour.

A

Instrument

Instrument-panel precision: hairlines, monospace metadata, restrained colour, an oscilloscope-grade graph. The strongest rival — its full argument is preserved in the archived document.

Risk: austerity is a crowded register — cool greys and mono chrome sit close to every dev-tool brand of the last five years. Rigorous, but not necessarily his.

Chosen
B

Atlas

Cartographic and archival: paper-warm neutrals, ink text, a serif display, and the graph drawn as a survey — graticule, plotted routes, place-name labels.

Why: it's the reading only Jason can own. Nobody else's portfolio is an atlas, because nobody else's work is territories.

C

Signal

Brutalist graph-native. Agreed weakest in both documents: boldest, but tips into gimmick and taxes long-form legibility. Eliminated.

The stance → Atlas

Atlas, because it's the reading that is specific to this body of work. Instrument proves rigour but borrows a register any dev-tool brand can claim; Atlas claims one nobody else can — a director who builds worlds and then surveys them. Precision doesn't leave, it changes instrument: from the oscilloscope to the theodolite. The test for every element: would it look at home on a well-made survey sheet? If not, it's decoration.

03 — Type

One new face, and it earns its dependency.

Atlas needs a serif with archival authority, and none of the four installed packages is one — this is the single case where "something new, if clearly better" applies. Source Serif 4 for display; IBM Plex Sans and JetBrains Mono stay from the installed set. Sora and Space Grotesk are retired.

04 — Motion

Plates turn; they don't bounce.

Atlas motion is slower and more symmetric than Instrument's — an ease-in-out, like a page turning or a survey table sliding into view. Same discipline: one curve, a four-step scale, a hard rule. Hover the chips to feel it.

Standard ease
cubic-bezier(.45, .05, .25, 1)

Gathers, travels, settles. Deliberate in, damped out — the weight of a physical plate, no overshoot.

Duration scale — hover each
micro 160ms
base 240ms
deliberate 400ms
plate 600ms
Earns motion

The same continuity rule as Instrument: graph relayout, theme flip, hover/dim, modal open, the graph settling on load. Atlas adds one: route drawing — a plotted edge may draw in along its path (once, on first reveal), because that's how a route is inked.

Stays static

Scroll reveals, decorative loops, text fades, parallax. A map does not fidget. If motion isn't carrying information, it's cut.

prefers-reduced-motion: reduce

Identical mechanism to Instrument: a single --motion-scale: 0 switch collapses every duration token to 0ms; transforms become instant opacity swaps; the force simulation ticks synchronously to steady state; route drawing renders fully inked.

05 — Graph signature · the centrepiece

Draw it as a survey, not a simulation.

The Atlas signature: a dotted graticule instead of blank canvas, nodes as surveyed marks (ring + centre point) rather than filled blobs, edges as plotted routes, and — the ownable move — theme territories drawn as soft tinted regions with italic serif names, like kingdoms on a historical map.

/map · relationships focus: WYRD

Nodes

Survey marks: an open ring with a centre point, not a filled blob. Ring radius keeps the existing formula r = 8 + 17.5·√(w/max); stroke = status colour; hubs get a second outer ring. Recency maps to ring opacity 0.55→1.0.

Edges — routes

Always curved (quadratic, slight bow) — routes, not wires. Extraction = solid 2px ink; related/theme = 1.5px dashed 5 4; lineage arrows become route arrowheads. Category colour from the existing edge tokens, unchanged — edges stay pure Reasonable Colors.

Territories & labels

Theme clusters get a convex-hull region: 7% tint fill, dashed 1px boundary, and an italic serif territory name — the move that makes /map and ThemeTerritories unmistakably one system. Project labels stay mono micro-caps; standing set only, rest on hover.

Canvas & states

Dotted graticule on --color-surface-sunken. Focus swaps the mark to the accent double-ring and raises a leader-line annotation. Dim-others reuses the existing tokens: node 0.28, edge 0.08, label 0.32.

06 — Colour & theme

Paper and ink, in both themes.

The bigger colour change of the two directions: neutrals warm to paper (light) and lamplit dark (dark); primary shifts from azure to an ink-blue; the accent becomes an oxide red — the colour of survey marks. Everything still routes through the semantic aliases, and every text pairing keeps the Reasonable Colors shade-difference rule (diff ≥3 for body, AA). Flip the toggle, top-right, to check the dark plate.

--color-accent · cinnamon-4

Staying inside Reasonable Colors

No hand-picked neutrals. Every warm neutral is derived from its Reasonable Colors counterpart by one rule: an oklab color-mix of the grey with the same-shade cinnamon, at a single --warmth ratio (12%). Because both inputs share a shade, the mix stays in that shade's lightness band — so the shade-difference contrast rule (diff 3 ≈ 4.5:1) keeps holding, and the whole ramp warms or cools from one knob.

The chromatic tokens never leave RC at all: primary = --color-blue-4 (azure → blue is the ink shift), accent = --color-cinnamon-4 (oxide). Cinnamon is currently claimed by the interactive-fiction theme edge — reassign that edge to the unclaimed --color-red-4 so no edge shares a hue with the accent. The dark theme pays nothing new either: its surfaces are already hand-set hexes in tokens.css, and Atlas just applies the same mix rule to those existing values (color-mix(in oklab, #1a1b1e 88%, var(--color-cinnamon-6))). And since derivation is now a rule rather than taste, assert it: a Vitest data test that resolves each text/surface pair and checks the WCAG ratio at build time, alongside the existing structural tests.

07 — Implementation

Enough to build from, no follow-up needed.

/* tokens.css — Atlas values */
--font-display: 'Source Serif 4 Variable', serif;   /* new dep */
--font-sans:    'IBM Plex Sans', system-ui, sans-serif;
--font-mono:    'JetBrains Mono', monospace;

/* paper neutrals — derived, never hand-picked */
--warmth: 12%;  /* the one knob */
--color-surface:        color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-gray-1) calc(100% - var(--warmth)), var(--color-cinnamon-1));
--color-surface-sunken: color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-gray-2) calc(100% - var(--warmth)), var(--color-cinnamon-2));
--color-border:         color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-gray-3) calc(100% - var(--warmth)), var(--color-cinnamon-3));
--color-text:           color-mix(in oklab, var(--color-gray-6) calc(100% - var(--warmth)), var(--color-cinnamon-6));

/* chromatic tokens — pure Reasonable Colors */
--color-primary: var(--color-blue-4);     /* ink */
--color-accent:  var(--color-cinnamon-4);  /* oxide; move IF theme edge → red-4 */

--ease-standard: cubic-bezier(.45, .05, .25, 1);
--motion-scale: 1;  /* → 0 under prefers-reduced-motion */
--dur-micro: calc(160ms * var(--motion-scale));   --dur-base: calc(240ms * var(--motion-scale));
--dur-deliberate: calc(400ms * var(--motion-scale)); --dur-plate: calc(600ms * var(--motion-scale));
